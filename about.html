Background

I've held senior operational leadership roles across technology companies, consulting organizations, and mission-driven institutions — working closely with founders and executive teams to build the systems that support organizational growth, distributed teams, and complex stakeholder environments.

That work has consistently centered on one problem: the gap between what leadership intends and what the organization actually does. The tools for closing that gap — clear expectations, visible milestones, documented accountability, structured governance — are what I design and build.

Performance management, to me, is not an HR process. It's a governance tool. The goal isn't better annual reviews. It's systems that make accountability consistent, defensible, and durable — regardless of who's in the room.

How I Work

Every engagement is built to outlast my involvement. I'm not building dependency — I'm building capability. That means clear documentation, shared ownership, and systems your team can run, adapt, and trust on their own.

I bring the same rigor I'd apply in a for-profit growth environment to nonprofit and association work — while recognizing the distinct governance structures, volunteer leadership dynamics, and resource constraints that shape those organizations.

How I Think

Operating principles

Clarity before evaluation

Expectations must be defined before performance is assessed. Accountability without prior clarity isn't accountability — it's judgment.

Documentation before consequence

Accountability requires shared understanding on record. If it wasn't written down, it didn't happen in any meaningful governance sense.

Milestones before judgment

Progress should be visible long before formal reviews. Surprises at review time mean the system failed earlier.

Continuity over personality

Systems should function regardless of who's in the role. If the organization depends on one person's knowledge, it's fragile.
